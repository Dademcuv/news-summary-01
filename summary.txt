Russia and Ukraine agreed a major prisoner swap as two days of U.S.-mediated talks ended in Abu Dhabi, Reuters reported, but differences remained on territory and no ceasefire was announced.

Iran designated Argentina's navy and air force as "terrorist organizations" in a reciprocal move after Buenos Aires blacklisted Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps, Reuters reported. Argentina said it "categorically rejects" the move.

NATO jets were scrambled twice overnight after suspected drone incursions from Belarus triggered air alerts across Lithuania and closed two airports, LRT reported; at least one drone crossed into Latvia.

U.N. chief Antonio Guterres told leaders at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya that "the climate crisis is outrunning us," Al Jazeera reported, urging a faster fossil-fuel phase-out; the U.S. skipped the talks for a second year.

Hundreds of thousands of workers joined Spain's first general strike in 14 years over housing and living costs, the BBC reported, with Barcelona's public transport at a standstill and about 60 arrests nationwide.

British counterterrorism police arrested a 41-year-old U.S. Army soldier in England on suspicion of spying for Russia, the BBC reported.

Nvidia's board met late on Wednesday to decide CEO Jensen Huang's future amid scrutiny of his alleged ties to a suspected Chinese intelligence operative, Reuters reported, citing a source; shares fell sharply after hours.

The Associated Press called Mississippi's U.S. Senate race for Democrat Scott Colom, who beat incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith to become the state's first Black senator since Reconstruction; Hyde-Smith conceded.

European stocks closed at a record high and U.S. shares rallied on AI optimism, Reuters reported, while the dollar hit a 19-month high.

Fitch put Paraguay's BB+ rating on watch for a possible downgrade after lender ueno collapsed, Reuters reported.
